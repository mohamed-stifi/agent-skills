# Story and script

## What sells (applies to every type)
1. **Sell the outcome, prove it with mechanism.** The viewer buys "my team gets its week back", not "has a policy engine".
   Features appear only as *proof* of the outcome.
2. **Show their world first.** Open on something the buyer recognises from their own day (a calendar, an alert at
   night, a queue, a spreadsheet). The best hook is their pain, specific: a time, a name, a number.
3. **One bookend.** The opening image comes back at the end, inverted (the week eaten → the week given back). This is the
   single strongest persuasion device; design both scenes together.
4. **One chapter = one proof moment = one benefit headline (≤ 6 words).**
5. **Trust before the promise** for serious buyers (banks, health, government, infra): control, approval, audit,
   on-premises, data stays: whatever the product really offers.
6. **The CTA is one action.** Default "Request a demo →". Use the user's CTA text and URL if given; never invent a URL.

## Facts discipline
- Every number, name, customer, metric and claim on screen or in the voice comes from `brief.md`'s facts bank, which
  cites a source you actually read. No invented percentages, "hours saved", logos or testimonials.
- Example data (fictional company names, ids, timestamps) is fine when the source itself uses examples, or when it is
  plainly illustrative; never present invented data as a real customer result.
- When the source has no numbers, sell with concrete *moments* instead (a time, a step, a decision), not with made-up stats.

## The voice-over script
- Speak **with** the picture, not over it: the line lands as its visual lands (±0.3 s). Start times = scene start +
  frame/60 of the beat (see `timing.at`).
- ~2.4–2.8 words per second at speed 1.0. Leave 0.3 s+ between lines; let decisive moments breathe (no voice on a snap).
- Short sentences. Rhythm of three ("Reruns. Tickets. Cost reports."). The promise line is said exactly as written on screen.
- Do not read the screen word for word except the headline of the promise; add what the picture cannot say (why it matters).
- `text` = the caption (digits, exact names). `say` = what the voice reads (spelled-out numbers, respellings).
  Example: `text: '04:52'`, `say: 'four fifty-two'`; `text: '1,284 jobs'`, `say: 'twelve hundred jobs'` (and then show `1,200+` in text).

## Default script skeleton for a 58 s promo (seconds)
| Beat | Time | Voice (example shape) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0.4–6 | three chores in rhythm, then "Your best people spend their week on this." |
| Problem | 6.5–13.5 | the scale (one or two real numbers), then the dilemma in two short lines |
| Turn | 14.5–18 | "Meet <Product>. The <category> that <outcome>, <key trust phrase>." |
| Chapters ×4 | 19–43 | one sentence each, ending on the benefit |
| Trust | 43–48 | "You choose …" / control in one line |
| Promise | 48.5–55 | the bookend sentence, = the on-screen headline |
| CTA | 55.5–57 | "Request a demo." |
